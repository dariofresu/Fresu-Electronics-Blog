Stitching Vias in Multilayer PCB Stackups and Their Role in Reducing EMI

This article explores the role of stitching vias in maintaining signal integrity and electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) as signals transition between layers in a PCB stackup. We will examine how the number and placement of stitching vias can influence the behavior of electromagnetic fields, and how proper design can prevent unwanted signal degradation and interference. Through simulation and comparison of different configurations, we will demonstrate how stitching vias contribute to the overall efficiency and reliability of PCB designs, ensuring signals travel cleanly from one layer to the next while keeping fields under control.

In modern PCB design, managing signal integrity and minimizing electromagnetic interference (EMI) are essential for ensuring optimal performance and reliability, especially as circuit speeds continue to increase. One of the key components in achieving this is the use of stitching vias. These small yet crucial features connect different layers of a PCB to the return reference plane, helping to control the flow of return currents and stabilize electromagnetic fields during signal transitions.

They serve three essential purposes:

  1. Providing a path for the return current to flow back to its source – This ensures a continuous and defined current loop, which is critical for maintaining the integrity of high-speed signals.

  2. Serving as a stable reference for the electric field – By acting as a reliable reference point, stitching vias help stabilize field behavior and support proper signal propagation.

  3. Maintaining equipotential across multiple metal structures – By connecting these structures with a low-impedance path, stitching vias help equalize potential differences, improving the overall electromagnetic performance of the PCB.

Together, these functions play a vital role in maintaining signal integrity and minimizing electromagnetic interference within the PCB. The significance of stitching vias becomes clear when analyzing their impact on electromagnetic field behavior during layer transitions.

With the help of Simbeor ® a simulation tool developed by simberian.com, it is possible to visualize how electromagnetic fields behave under different conditions, specifically, when two stitching vias are used compared to four. This analysis reveals how design choices affect the containment and propagation of electromagnetic fields.

In our scenario, a multilayer PCB stackup with several planes is examined. Here, electromagnetic fields spread in the cavity between the planes.

In the first, non-optimal scenario (left side of Figure 1), as signals transition between layers, the electromagnetic fields are confined by only two stitching vias. This limited number of vias allows the fields to propagate freely within the stackup, leading to uncontrolled energy dispersal.

By contrast, the second scenario (right side of Figure 1) involves the placement of four stitching vias around the signal path. These vias provide a 360-degree reference for the signal during its transition, containing the fields within a defined area, as seen in Figure 2.

This setup prevents the uncontrolled spreading seen in the first scenario (left side of Figure 2) and ensures the electromagnetic fields remain localized. Such control over field behavior is essential for improving the performance and reliability of PCB designs.

When signals transition between layers, they require both a clear return current path and a stable reference for the electric fields. Proper design of these elements is critical for maintaining signal integrity and addressing electromagnetic compatibility (EMC). This makes it vital to engineer both the return path and the field reference to prevent disruptions and limit field spreading.

In the optimized design (right side of Figure 2), four stitching vias are strategically placed around the signal path. These vias ensure a continuous reference for the electric fields as the signal transitions between layers, confining the fields to the return plane and preventing unwanted propagation. This behavior ensures that the fields remain controlled even as the signal transitions along the trace.

Without these stitching vias, fields would begin to spread uncontrollably in between the layers as the signal transitions from horizontal to vertical paths. This spreading is particularly problematic at high frequencies, such as 30 GHz, where precise control over field containment is critical to maintaining proper signal behavior. At these frequencies, even minor field disruptions can cause significant performance issues, including signal degradation and unintended radiation.

However, effective containment and proper return path design are not limited to high-speed signals. Lower-frequency signals also benefit from careful engineering, as these principles help maintain signal integrity and EMC across a broad range of operating conditions. Regardless of the signal speed, ensuring controlled transitions between layers is essential for achieving reliable and efficient PCB performance.

Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates that the number and placement of stitching vias significantly impact the behavior of electromagnetic fields during layer transitions. In the non-optimal scenario with only two stitching vias, the fields are allowed to spread uncontrollably, leading to potential signal integrity and EMC issues. However, by strategically placing four stitching vias, the fields are contained, preventing unwanted energy dispersal and ensuring better signal quality and reliability.

While the need for proper field containment is especially critical for high-speed signals, the principles of effective stitching via placement apply to all signal frequencies. Careful engineering of the return path and field reference during layer transitions is essential for achieving optimal PCB performance and minimizing electromagnetic interference across a wide range of operating conditions.
